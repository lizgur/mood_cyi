**Bitcoin first traded at $1,000 on 27 November 2013, on Mt. Gox, which was then the world's largest bitcoin-to-dollar exchange.**[^1] CoinDesk reported it that morning, at 9:52 a.m. US Eastern time, as "a whole new milestone," noting that it was the first time a US dollar exchange rate had reached $1,000.[^1] There's one footnote to that "first." On 19 November, the Chinese exchange BTC China had briefly traded above the equivalent of $1,000 in yuan.[^1][^2] The rally that got bitcoin there had doubled the price in about a week,[^1] and in the days around a US Senate hearing it had swung by hundreds of dollars.[^2][^3] Two days later, bitcoin cost more than an ounce of gold on Mt. Gox.[^5] A week after that, China's central bank barred its banks from bitcoin business, and the price dropped back below $1,000.[^6][^8]

## When did bitcoin first reach $1,000?

**27 November 2013, on Mt. Gox.** CoinDesk's Garrick Hileman wrote that the price on Mt. Gox, "the world's largest USD-BTC exchange," had reached $1,000. It was the first time that level had been reached in a US dollar exchange rate.[^1]

**Why some sources say 28 November.** The BBC, writing on 5 December, gave the date as 28 November.[^6] CoinDesk's report is dated 27 November, 9:52 a.m. EST, and it describes the $1,000 price in the present tense, so we use 27 November.[^1] A dated news report from the day itself is the best evidence we have. Exchange time zones and differences between exchanges can easily move a date like this by one day.

**Mt. Gox, not "the price."** In 2013 there was no single bitcoin price. Exchanges traded at noticeably different levels. On 18 November, CoinDesk noted a gap of more than $80 between Mt. Gox and the cheaper Bitstamp and BTC-e exchanges.[^2] On the morning of the $1,000 milestone, CoinDesk's own Bitcoin Price Index, an average of several exchanges, read $942.[^1] So "bitcoin hit $1,000" really means "bitcoin hit $1,000 on Mt. Gox." For how Mt. Gox started, see [the first Bitcoin exchanges of 2010](/blog/first-bitcoin-exchange-2010).

## Didn't BTC China get there first?

In yuan terms, yes, briefly. On 19 November 2013, BTC China's price reached 6,989 CNY, about $1,147, at around 01:00 GMT. Within seconds it crashed to almost 60% of that, then recovered to about $850.[^2][^3] At around the same time Mt. Gox peaked at $900.[^2] CoinDesk's 27 November report treats this the same way we do: BTC China "briefly topped the equivalent of $1,000" on 19 November, but Mt. Gox was the first time a US dollar rate reached it.[^1]

By then BTC China was big. On 18 November, CoinDesk called it "the world's largest bitcoin exchange by trade volume."[^2] In December, CNBC said it was the world's biggest bitcoin exchange, citing Bitcoinity.org, with about 30% of the market's volume the week before.[^8]

## How bitcoin got from $200 to $1,000 in a month

Here is the November 2013 run as CoinDesk reported it at the time:[^2]

- **Late October:** the price broke through $200.
- **6 November:** $300.
- **15 November:** $400 on all exchanges.
- **17 November, 11:50 GMT:** $500 on Mt. Gox. CoinDesk called it "a 50x increase in just 12 months."
- **18 November:** above $600 on Mt. Gox in the morning, then $750 by the evening, during the Senate hearing.
- **19 November, about 01:15 GMT:** $900 on Mt. Gox, then back to about $670 later that morning.

For scale, CBC reported that on 29 October, when [the first bitcoin ATM](/blog/first-bitcoin-atm-2013) opened in Vancouver, its operator was selling bitcoin for $207.37 to $211.32.[^9] Bitcoin had also had a sharp spring in 2013. CoinDesk recalled an April peak of $266 followed by a plunge to $65 the same week.[^2]

The $1,000 day also came almost exactly a year after [the first halving](/blog/first-bitcoin-halving-2012). Block 210,000, which cut the block reward from 50 to 25 BTC, was mined on 28 November 2012 at 15:24 UTC.[^10]

## The Senate hearing of 18 November 2013

On Monday 18 November 2013, the US Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs held what the Guardian called the Senate's first hearing on bitcoin. It was titled "Beyond Silk Road: Potential Risks, Threats and Promises of Virtual Currencies."[^4] Silk Road, an online black market that used bitcoin, had been closed by the FBI in October.[^4]

The tone was calmer than many expected:

- **The Fed chair's letter.** Before the hearing, Ben Bernanke wrote to the committee that virtual currencies "may hold long-term promise, particularly if the innovations promote a faster, more secure and more efficient payment system." He added that the Fed did not necessarily have authority to regulate them directly.[^4]
- **The Justice Department.** Acting assistant attorney general Mythili Raman said virtual currencies "offer legitimate financial services," while warning that some features "appeal to criminals."[^4]
- **The bitcoin side.** Jerry Brito of the Mercatus Center and Patrick Murck of the Bitcoin Foundation testified. Murck said bitcoin "is not a magic cloaking device that allows criminal actors free reign."[^4] CoinDesk reported that Jeremy Allaire also testified, and that many in the bitcoin community were "impressed and even delighted" by how Senator Thomas Carper handled the hearing.[^3]

The price moved with it. The Guardian reported bitcoin topping $700 on Mt. Gox as the hearing began.[^4] CoinDesk later wrote that its price index went from $478 on the morning of 18 November to $744 at midnight GMT.[^1]

## After $1,000: more than an ounce of gold, then China

**29 November: worth more than gold.** Two days after $1,000, bitcoin reached $1,242 on Mt. Gox. That was just above the gold price at the time, $1,241.98 an ounce. CoinDesk put bitcoin's market capitalisation that day at around $13.5 billion.[^5] The price had dropped back to $1,146 on Mt. Gox by the time CoinDesk published.[^5]

**5 December: China bars its banks.** The People's Bank of China and four other regulators (the industry and IT ministry, and the banking, securities and insurance regulators) issued a joint notice, Yin Fa [2013] No. 289. The text is dated 3 December 2013 and was published on 5 December.[^7] It said bitcoin was "a specific virtual commodity" with no legal status as money. It barred financial and payment institutions from pricing in bitcoin, trading it, or offering related services. Ordinary people, it said, remained free to take part "at their own risk."[^7][^6] The BBC reported that Chinese exchange prices fell after the announcement.[^6] CNBC reported a drop of about 20%, to $980 on Mt. Gox and $932 on CoinDesk's index, at midday London time.[^8] We'll cover this notice in full in its own post.

**The "bubble" debate.** Around the same time, former Fed chairman Alan Greenspan told Bloomberg that bitcoin was "a bubble," according to the BBC.[^6] We're not going to argue that either way here. This is a history post, not a price call.

## What happened to Mt. Gox?

Mt. Gox didn't last long after its $1,000 moment. On 28 February 2014, it applied for civil rehabilitation, a form of bankruptcy protection, at the Tokyo District Court.[^11] In its announcement the company said that about 750,000 bitcoins deposited by users and about 100,000 of its own bitcoins had disappeared, and that it had closed its site at noon on 25 February, Japan time.[^11] The Guardian reported that it had 127,000 creditors and blamed "a weakness in our system" for the collapse.[^12]

## $1,000 compared with 2009 and 2010

- **October 2009:** the first published dollar exchange rate, from NewLibertyStandard, was **$1 = 1,309.03 BTC** on 5 October 2009.[^13] At $1,000 per coin, the same $1 would have bought about 0.001 BTC. See [Bitcoin's first price](/blog/first-bitcoin-price-2009).
- **May 2010:** Laszlo Hanyecz offered "10,000 bitcoins for a couple of pizzas" on 18 May 2010. On 22 May he reported, "I successfully traded 10,000 bitcoins for pizza."[^14] At $1,000 a coin, those 10,000 BTC would have been worth $10 million. That's our arithmetic, not a market valuation. The full story is in [Bitcoin Pizza Day](/blog/bitcoin-pizza-day-transaction).
- **November 2013:** $1,000 for one bitcoin on Mt. Gox, 1,285 days after the pizza and 1,514 days after NewLibertyStandard's first rate.[^1][^13][^14]

## Why the first $1,000 matters

- **It put bitcoin in front of a mainstream audience.** In one month, bitcoin had a Senate hearing,[^4] a letter from the Fed chair,[^4] and a central-bank notice in China.[^7]
- **It showed bitcoin's price was a patchwork.** Different exchanges and currencies gave different "first $1,000" dates.[^1][^2][^6]
- **It's a reminder of exchange risk.** The exchange where bitcoin first hit $1,000 was in bankruptcy proceedings three months later.[^11][^12]
