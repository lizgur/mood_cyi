**BitcoinTalk was founded on 22 November 2009.** Satoshi Nakamoto opened it with the post "Welcome to the new Bitcoin forum!" at 18:04:28 UTC that day. The forum ran on SMF (Simple Machines Forum) software at bitcoin.org/smf, on hosting provided by Martti Malmi ("sirius"), and replaced an earlier SourceForge forum. It moved to bitcointalk.org in July 2011 and is still running. Sunday 22 November 2026 is its 17th anniversary.

## What did Satoshi's first BitcoinTalk post say?

The whole post is short. Satoshi wrote "Welcome to the new Bitcoin forum!", said the old forum could still be reached at bitcoin.sourceforge.net, and promised to "repost some selected threads here and add updated answers to questions where I can." He linked the FAQ and the SourceForge download page.

He kept that promise right away. Within 28 minutes he posted "Repost: Bitcoin Maturation" (18:31:44 UTC) and "Repost: Request: Make this anonymous?" (18:32:00 UTC), both moved over from the old forum.

## Why is the first BitcoinTalk thread topic 5?

The welcome thread lives at bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=5, not topic=1. In a 2012 reply in that thread, administrator theymos wrote that topics 1, 2 and 4 sit in the staff forum, and topic 3 was permanently deleted. That is his account; nobody outside the staff can check it.

The Bitcoin Wiki quotes an early admin-side note from Satoshi about setting up the board: he left the admin account on the original SMF theme "so if I somehow completely wedge the custom theme I can still get in to fix it," planned a "neat little 12x12 coin image" to replace the forum's star icons, and noted that the Announcements board was moderator-only.

## Who ran BitcoinTalk?

- **Satoshi Nakamoto** started the forum and customised the SMF software and theme.
- **Martti Malmi ("sirius")** provided the hosting. The Bitcoin Wiki calls him the second Bitcoin developer after Satoshi and the founder of the Bitcoin Forum, and says he still manages the bitcointalk.org domain.
- **theymos** is the administrator today.

## How BitcoinTalk moved: bitcoin.org/smf to bitcointalk.org

1. **2009:** Satoshi uses a forum on SourceForge. It has not survived.
2. **22 Nov 2009:** The new forum opens at bitcoin.org/smf.
3. **Later:** It moves to forum.bitcoin.org.
4. **July 2011:** It moves to bitcointalk.org "in order to make it explicitly unofficial", and bitcoin.org later removes its forum link.
5. **22 July 2012:** The forum reaches its one millionth post.

## What happened on BitcoinTalk in its first year?

Most of early Bitcoin's public life ran through this one forum. A few dated examples:

- **16 Dec 2009:** Satoshi announces "Bitcoin 0.2 released!" there.
- **18 May 2010, 00:35:20 UTC:** Laszlo Hanyecz posts "Pizza for bitcoins?": "I'll pay 10,000 bitcoins for a couple of pizzas.. like maybe 2 large ones so I have some left over for the next day." Read the full story in [Bitcoin Pizza Day: the 10,000 BTC transaction](/blog/bitcoin-pizza-day-transaction) and [who Laszlo Hanyecz is](/blog/laszlo-hanyecz).
- **22 May 2010, 19:17:26 UTC:** In the same thread, Laszlo reports: "I just want to report that I successfully traded 10,000 bitcoins for pizza." sirius replies: "Congratulations laszlo, a great milestone reached."
- **27 Nov 2010:** The thread for Slush's pool, the [first Bitcoin mining pool](/blog/first-bitcoin-mining-pool-slush-2010), starts.
- **12 Dec 2010:** In the Satoshi Nakamoto Institute's list of threads Satoshi started, the last one is "Added some DoS limits, removed safe mode (0.3.19)." That was a day after his [WikiLeaks "hornet's nest" warning](/blog/satoshi-wikileaks-hornets-nest-2010). What came next is in [Satoshi's last message](/blog/satoshi-last-message-2011).

The Pizza Day offer went up 177 days after the forum opened, and the "done" post came 181 days after (both counted from the dates above).

## Can you still read Satoshi's BitcoinTalk posts?

Yes. The original welcome thread is still live at bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=5. The Satoshi Nakamoto Institute keeps a dated archive of every thread Satoshi posted in.
