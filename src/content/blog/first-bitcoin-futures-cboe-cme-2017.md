**The first bitcoin futures on a major U.S. exchange began trading at 5:00 p.m. Chicago time on Sunday, 10 December 2017, on the Cboe Futures Exchange (CFE), under the ticker XBT.**[^5][^7][^10] Each XBT contract was worth 1 bitcoin.[^9] A week later, on Sunday 17 December 2017, CME Group launched its own contract, ticker BTC, worth 5 bitcoin each.[^2][^4] Both had been self-certified with the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) on 1 December.[^1] Cboe's first day drew 4,127 contracts and so much traffic that its website slowed down.[^8][^10] By March 2019 Cboe had stopped adding new bitcoin contracts, while CME kept going.[^14][^15]

## 1 December 2017: the CFTC statement

On Friday 1 December 2017, the CFTC announced that CME and the Cboe Futures Exchange had self-certified new bitcoin futures contracts. Cantor Exchange self-certified bitcoin binary options the same day.[^1]

Self-certification means the exchange files the product itself. The CFTC was clear that this "is not a Commission approval" and not an endorsement of virtual currency products.[^1] CFTC Chairman J. Christopher Giancarlo said: "Bitcoin, a virtual currency, is a commodity unlike any the Commission has dealt with in the past."[^1] The agency said its staff had talked with CME for six weeks and with CFE for four months, and that the exchanges agreed to changes in contract design, settlement and (for CME) margin.[^1] It also warned about the "potentially high level of volatility and risk in trading these contracts."[^1]

CME announced the same day that its contract would launch on Sunday 17 December for a trade date of Monday 18 December, with an initial margin of 35%.[^2] Its CFTC filing noted: "There is currently no designated contract market offering a futures contract on bitcoin."[^3]

On Monday 4 December, Cboe set its own date: 5:00 p.m. CT on Sunday 10 December, a week ahead of CME, with trading fees waived through December.[^5]

## 10 December 2017: Cboe XBT opens

Cboe said XBT "began trading exactly as planned at 5:00 p.m. CT on Sunday, December 10, 2017."[^7] Gemini, whose auction price the contract settled against, gave the same time as 6:00 p.m. Eastern.[^6]

- **The website, not the market, struggled.** Cboe posted on the launch night: "Due to heavy traffic on our website, visitors to http://www.cboe.com may find that it is performing slower than usual and may at times be temporarily unavailable... All trading systems are operating normally."[^10]
- **Two trading halts.** CNBC reported that about two hours after launch the futures were up 10%, triggering a two-minute halt. By 10:05 p.m. ET they were up 20%, triggering a five-minute halt.[^10]
- **The first session.** It closed at 3:15 p.m. CT on Monday 11 December with 4,127 contracts traded and about 20 trading firms taking part. The January contract settled at $18,545.[^8] CNBC put that at 19.9% higher.[^10]
- **Spot prices for context.** Shortly before launch, bitcoin traded at $14,950.06 on Coinbase, per CNBC. On Monday, CoinDesk's index had it at $17,261.[^10]

CNBC called it "the first futures contract on an established exchange" and said the debut was "relatively orderly."[^10] Not every broker joined in. Interactive Brokers said its clients could not take short positions, and CNBC reported that some of the largest futures brokers offered limited or no support at first.[^10]

## 17 December 2017: CME BTC opens

CME's BTC contract listed on CME Globex effective Sunday 17 December 2017, for trade date Monday 18 December.[^4] Its trading hours were 5:00 p.m. to 4:00 p.m. Central Time, Sunday to Friday.[^4]

- **5 bitcoin per contract**, settled in cash against the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate.[^3][^4] In December 2017 the BRR took its prices from Bitstamp, GDAX, itBit and Kraken.[^2]
- **Price limits** of 7%, 13% and 20% around a reference price. Trading was not allowed beyond 20%.[^4]
- **First months listed:** January, February, March and June 2018.[^4]

CNBC reported that CME's January contract opened at $20,650 and settled 2.05% lower on Monday, at $19,100. By late Monday, about 1,049 January contracts had traded (the equivalent of 5,245 bitcoin), against roughly 3,860 Cboe contracts.[^11] Bitcoin itself fell about 2% to $18,718 on CoinDesk's index that day.[^11]

## Who regulated what?

The futures sat with the CFTC. On 11 December, SEC Chairman Jay Clayton wrote that the CFTC had designated bitcoin a commodity, and that "the regulation of commodity futures tied directly to bitcoin" was within the CFTC's purview.[^12] In the same statement he said the SEC had not approved any exchange-traded products, such as ETFs, holding cryptocurrencies.[^12]

## Did futures pop the 2017 bubble?

That's a claim, not a settled fact, so here is who made it.

In May 2018, four economists writing in a San Francisco Fed *Economic Letter* (Galina Hale, Arvind Krishnamurthy, Marianna Kudlyak and Patrick Shultz) noted that bitcoin peaked at $19,511 on 17 December 2017, by their Bloomberg data, the same day CME futures started trading.[^13] They argued this "does not appear to be a coincidence." In their view, before futures only optimists could easily bet on bitcoin. Futures let pessimists bet against it too, which they said "contributed to the reversal" of the price.[^13] They also wrote that trading was thin on Cboe until CME joined, and that average daily volume in the month after CME's launch was about six times larger than when only Cboe offered futures.[^13]

That's their argument, not ours. We don't forecast prices. For where bitcoin's price story began, see [the first bitcoin price in 2009](/blog/first-bitcoin-price-2009) and [when bitcoin first hit $1,000 in 2013](/blog/bitcoin-1000-dollars-2013).

## March 2019: Cboe steps back

On 14 March 2019, CFE told traders it was not adding an XBT contract for March 2019: "CFE is assessing its approach with respect to how it plans to continue to offer digital asset derivatives for trading. While it considers its next steps, CFE does not currently intend to list additional XBT futures contracts for trading."[^14] Contracts already listed could still be traded, and CoinDesk noted the last one, XBTM19, would expire in June 2019.[^14]

CNBC reported that CME had announced no changes, and that CME's 30-day average bitcoin futures volume was more than four times Cboe's.[^15] So the first bitcoin futures contract ended up shorter-lived than the second.

## Why the first bitcoin futures matter

- **Bitcoin got a Chicago futures price.** Bitcoin's first exchanges appeared in 2010 (see [the first bitcoin exchange](/blog/first-bitcoin-exchange-2010)). By December 2017, Cboe and CME, which CNBC called the world's largest futures exchange, were listing bitcoin futures under CFTC oversight.[^1][^5][^2][^11]
- **A different regulatory answer from China's.** In 2013 China's central bank told its banks to stay away from bitcoin ([China's 2013 ban](/blog/china-bitcoin-ban-december-2013)). Four years later, the U.S. regulator described bitcoin as "a commodity unlike any" it had dealt with, and let exchanges list futures under its rules.[^1]
- **A two-sided market.** The SF Fed authors wrote that before December 2017 there was no market for bitcoin derivatives, so betting on a fall was "extremely difficult, if not impossible." Futures changed that, which is the centre of their argument.[^13]
