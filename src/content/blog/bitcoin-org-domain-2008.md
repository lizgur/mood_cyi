**bitcoin.org was registered on 18 August 2008.** The registry record for the domain gives the registration time as 13:19:55 UTC that day. That is 74 days before Satoshi Nakamoto published the Bitcoin Whitepaper on 31 October 2008, and the paper's first public link pointed straight to it: `http://www.bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf`. It is the earliest dated public trace of Bitcoin.

## What happened on 18 August 2008

Nothing public, at first. The domain was registered, and that's all the record shows. No forum post, no code and no paper went with it. The Bitcoin Wiki's timeline lists it as the first 2008 entry, followed four days later by Satoshi contacting Wei Dai, creator of b-money, on 22 August 2008 after a referral from Adam Back.

So when you put the dates in order, the website name came before almost everything else we can see.

## The Whitepaper's first home

On 31 October 2008 Satoshi posted to the Cryptography mailing list: "I've been working on a new electronic cash system that's fully peer-to-peer, with no trusted third party. The paper is available at: http://www.bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf". The same post ends with the link again: "Full paper at: http://www.bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf".

The PDF is still at bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf today. Our [Bitcoin Whitepaper explained](/blog/bitcoin-whitepaper-explained) guide walks through it section by section, and our [Whitepaper anniversary](/blog/bitcoin-whitepaper-anniversary) post covers 31 October itself.

## bitcoin.org and the v0.1 release

When the software shipped, the site got a job. Satoshi's [Bitcoin v0.1](/blog/bitcoin-v0-1-release-2009) announcement on 9 January 2009 said: "See bitcoin.org for screenshots." The download itself was hosted on SourceForge.

## Who owned bitcoin.org?

According to bitcoin.org's own "About" page, the domain "was originally registered and owned by Bitcoin's first two developers, Satoshi Nakamoto and Martti Malmi." Malmi, known on the forums as Sirius, was the second Bitcoin developer after Satoshi and was active from 2009, so he wasn't part of the project when the domain was registered in August 2008. He shared ownership later.

The same page says that "when Nakamoto left the project, he gave ownership of the domain to additional people, separate from the Bitcoin developers, to spread responsibility and prevent any one person or group from easily gaining control over the Bitcoin project." (We covered Satoshi's exit in [Satoshi's last message, 2011](/blog/satoshi-last-message-2011).)

From 2011 to 2013 the site was mainly used to release new versions of the software now called Bitcoin Core. It was redesigned in 2013, and developer documentation was added in 2014. Today the page lists a pseudonymous owner, Cøbra, and says: "Bitcoin.org is not Bitcoin's official website. Just like nobody owns the email technology, nobody owns the Bitcoin network."

## The Whitepaper court cases (2021 and 2024)

The domain's link to the Whitepaper ended up in court. On 28 June 2021 the High Court of England and Wales gave Craig Wright a default judgment against "the person or persons responsible for the operation and publication of the website www.bitcoin.org (including the person or persons using the pseudonym 'Cøbra')". The order barred the site from making the Whitepaper available in the United Kingdom. The claim was not defended.

Three years later, in a separate case brought by the Crypto Open Patent Alliance, Mr Justice Mellor announced on 14 March 2024 that Wright is not Satoshi Nakamoto. In his written judgment of 20 May 2024 he declared "that Dr Wright is not the author of the Bitcoin White Paper."

## How to check the registration date yourself

Look up `bitcoin.org` in any RDAP or WHOIS tool, such as ICANN Lookup (lookup.icann.org). Find the event marked **registration**: it reads 2008-08-18T13:19:55Z. The registrant's details are not public, so the record shows *when*, not *who*.

## Why it matters

A domain registration is a small thing. But it tells you Satoshi was planning a public project, with a name and a home, months before showing anyone the paper. And because the Whitepaper link pointed to bitcoin.org, that one URL ties the August 2008 domain to everything that followed.
