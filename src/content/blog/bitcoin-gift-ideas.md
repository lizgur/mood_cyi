**The best gift for a Bitcoiner who already has a hardware wallet, a stack of stickers and strong opinions is something tied to a real, dated moment in Bitcoin's history: the whitepaper (31 October 2008), the genesis block (3 January 2009) or Bitcoin Pizza Day (22 May 2010).** Those moments are fixed points anyone can check, so a gift built on one says "I know what this means to you" rather than "I saw an orange logo."

Below is how to match a moment to the person, what each MOOD shirt actually carries, and the practical bits (sizing, shipping from Cyprus, returns) that decide whether a gift lands on time and fits.

## Why history makes a better Bitcoin gift

Most Bitcoin merch is a logo or a price joke. Logos are everywhere, and price jokes age badly. A gift tied to a specific event has three advantages:

- **It's verifiable.** The whitepaper is a public PDF, the genesis block is block 0 on every explorer, and the pizza transaction has a hash you can look up.
- **It starts conversations.** "What's that text on your shirt?" is an easy opening to explain why Bitcoin exists.
- **It doesn't broadcast holdings.** A historical reference says you care about Bitcoin, not how much you own.

## Match the moment to the person

### For the reader and builder: the Bitcoin Whitepaper (31 October 2008)

If they quote Satoshi, run a node, or have actually read *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*, this is their moment. Satoshi Nakamoto shared the nine-page paper on a cryptography mailing list on 31 October 2008, and the date is now marked every year as Whitepaper Day.

**The gift:** the [MOOD Bitcoin Whitepaper T-shirt](/products/whitepaper-t-shirt) carries an all-over print of the full original whitepaper, every line and every diagram, with a graffiti-style MOOD logo layered in. It's white, 100% cotton, €100.

Want to brief yourself before you hand it over? Our [section-by-section guide to the whitepaper](/blog/bitcoin-whitepaper-explained) covers it in plain English.

### For the sound-money believer: the Genesis Block (3 January 2009)

If they talk about inflation, bailouts and why Bitcoin was built, give them the line Satoshi put inside Bitcoin's very first block: "The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks." It's a real front-page headline from The Times that day, and it works as both a timestamp and a statement.

**The gift:** the [MOOD Genesis Block T-shirt](/products/genesis-t-shirt) prints that headline on the chest of a black, 100% cotton tee, with a "Code your identity" tagline inside. €70.

The story behind the headline is in [what the Genesis Block message means](/blog/genesis-block-message-chancellor-on-brink).

### For the meme-lover and storyteller: Bitcoin Pizza Day (22 May 2010)

If they love Bitcoin culture and its legends, this is the one. On 22 May 2010 Laszlo Hanyecz paid 10,000 BTC for two Papa John's pizzas after offering the deal on the Bitcointalk forum, one of the first known purchases of a physical good with bitcoin.

**The gift:** the [MOOD Pizza Day T-shirt](/products/pizza-t-shirt) is white, 100% cotton, with the real transaction hash from that 2010 purchase on the sleeve cuffs and mini pizzas on the sleeves. €70.

The on-chain details are in [our Bitcoin Pizza Day post](/blog/bitcoin-pizza-day-transaction).

### Not sure which one?

Ask yourself which story they'd tell at a meetup. If it's about how Bitcoin works, pick the Whitepaper. If it's about why it exists, pick the Genesis Block. If it's a funny story about how early it all was, pick Pizza Day. For the full background on all three, see our [Bitcoin history timeline 2008–2010](/blog/bitcoin-history-timeline-2008-2010).
