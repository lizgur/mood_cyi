**HODL started as a typo. On 18 December 2013, a BitcoinTalk user called GameKyuubi posted a thread titled "I AM HODLING" while the bitcoin price was crashing, and explained that he was holding because "I'm a bad trader and I KNOW I'M A BAD TRADER."**[^1] The forum shows the post at 10:03:03 AM, which Bitcoin Magazine gives as 10:03 UTC.[^1][^2] Three minutes later, another user replied with just "HODL!"[^1] The misspelling spread from there and now means holding bitcoin (or any asset) instead of selling, especially through a fall.[^5][^8] "Hold On for Dear Life" came later. It's a backronym, not where the word came from.[^6][^7]

## Who said HODL first?

**GameKyuubi, on BitcoinTalk, on 18 December 2013.** His thread "I AM HODLING" is where the spelling starts, and it's the origin that Know Your Meme, CoinDesk and Bitcoin Magazine all point to.[^6][^5][^2] He posted it in the Speculation board of the same forum Satoshi Nakamoto had opened four years earlier (see [when BitcoinTalk was founded](/blog/bitcointalk-forum-founded-2009)).[^1]

The first line of the post admits the typo. Here it is exactly as he wrote it, spacing and spelling included:[^1]

> "I type d that tyitle twice because I knew it was wrong the first time.  Still wrong.  w/e."

You'll often see this quoted as "I typed that title twice." That's a tidied-up version. CoinDesk and Bitcoin Magazine both quote the original "I type d that tyitle."[^5][^2]

Then the actual argument:[^1]

> "WHY AM I HOLDING? I'LL TELL YOU WHY.  It's because I'm a bad trader and I KNOW I'M A BAD TRADER."

He went on to say that good traders can "spot the highs and the lows," that he wasn't one of them, and that "You only sell in a bear market if you are a good day trader or an illusioned noob.  The people inbetween hold.  In a zero-sum game such as this, traders can only take your money if you sell."[^1]

And the sign-off that explains the spelling:[^1]

> "so i've had some whiskey
> actually on the bottle it's spelled whisky
> w/e
> sue me
> (but only if it's payable in BTC)"

Note that in the body of the post he spells it correctly: "WHY AM I HOLDING?" Only the title says HODLING.[^1]

## What was happening to the bitcoin price on 18 December 2013?

It was falling hard. GameKyuubi's own post says "BTC crashing."[^1] Prices differed a lot between exchanges in 2013, so here's what each report said:

- **Mt. Gox:** the BBC reported the rate falling "from $717 to as low as $480" in Wednesday's trading (18 December).[^3]
- **Bitstamp:** bitcoin fell as much as 43%, to $382.21, according to the Sydney Morning Herald.[^4]
- **BTC China:** down as much as 49%, to 2,011 yuan ($372.77), per the same report.[^4] The BBC quoted a low of 2,560 yuan ($421), citing the South China Morning Post, against an all-time high of 7,588 yuan ($1,250) in late November.[^3]
- **Over 24 hours:** Bitcoin Magazine later described a 39% fall, "from above $700 to under $500," before the post went up.[^2]

**Why it fell.** BTC China, which the BBC called "the world's biggest Bitcoin exchange in terms of trading volumes," said local payment companies had been blocked from providing it with clearing services, so it could no longer accept yuan deposits.[^3] This came after Chinese regulators had banned local banks from handling bitcoin transactions about two weeks earlier.[^3][^4] We cover that in [China's first bitcoin ban, December 2013](/blog/china-bitcoin-ban-december-2013).

It was a sharp turn from late November. The Sydney Morning Herald noted that bitcoin had topped $1,000 before dropping back,[^4] and the full story of that peak is in [when bitcoin first hit $1,000](/blog/bitcoin-1000-dollars-2013). You can see the mood in the thread itself. One early reply said, "I sodl half my coins at $730."[^1]

## How "HODL" became a meme

It caught on almost immediately:

- **10:06 UTC, three minutes after the post:** user elux replied "HODL!"[^1]
- **10:14 UTC:** user piramida replied with a graphic, which Bitcoin Magazine calls the start of the meme ("And the rest, as they say, is history").[^2][^1]
- **The same thread:** replies ran with the spelling, including "WE OL SHAL HODL!" and "Spartans Hodl."[^1] When we checked on 27 November 2026, the thread ran to 131 pages and the forum counted 797,080 reads.[^1]
- **20 July 2014:** according to Know Your Meme, a Reddit user asked "what's HODL?" on r/Bitcoin and another user pointed to the BitcoinTalk thread.[^6]
- **19 December 2015:** Urban Dictionary got an entry for "hodl," per Know Your Meme.[^6]
- **30 April 2017:** an explainer, "What's The Backstory On The Word, HODL?", was published on Medium (now on Hacker Noon).[^9]
- **December 2017:** Know Your Meme added its own "Hodl" entry on 22 December 2017.[^6]

CoinDesk summed it up in 2019: the misspelled "HODL" "quickly circulated among the crypto community" and became slang for holding bitcoin rather than selling it, and was later used for other cryptocurrencies too.[^5]

## What does HODL stand for?

**Nothing, originally.** It's a misspelling of "hold." Dictionary.com's etymology for *hodl* is "First recorded in 2013; misspelling of hold."[^8]

**"Hold On for Dear Life" is a backronym**, meaning the words were fitted to the letters after the fact. Know Your Meme says the backronym "was subsequently attributed to the term" after it became popular.[^6] Jameson Lopp puts it more bluntly: "HODL does NOT stand for 'Hold on for Dear Life' - that's just a backronym."[^7] The phrase was already being played with by April 2017, when the Medium explainer closed with "Hold Onto them for Dear Life."[^9] Some outlets now list "to hold on for dear life" as a second sense of the word, Bitcoin Magazine among them.[^2]

So if someone asks "what does HODL stand for?", the honest answer is a typo by a man who'd had some whisky. The acronym came later.

## Is HODL in the dictionary?

**Yes, in Dictionary.com.** It lists *hodl* (or *HODL*) as a verb, "to avoid selling (cryptocurrency, other digital assets, or stocks), especially over a long period of time," and as an adjective for a strategy of holding rather than selling for short-term gains.[^8]

**Not (yet) in Merriam-Webster.** When we checked on 27 November 2026, merriam-webster.com had no entry page for "hodl."[^10] We couldn't confirm either way for the Oxford English Dictionary or Collins, so we're not making a claim about them.

## Did GameKyuubi ever say more?

A little, and he's still pseudonymous:

- **2019:** CoinDesk said it had "tracked down Mike, the man who coined this term," to ask whether his views on trading had changed.[^5] "Mike" is the only name CoinDesk gives.
- **March 2026:** a post from the GameKyuubi account on BitcoinTalk recalled that "HODL started as a mistake I made when I was smashed out of my mind after work on a rainy night in the Japanese countryside." He wrote that he only found out how far the word had spread years later, when a coworker joked "HODL THE DOOR" about a Game of Thrones episode.[^11] The same post announced a HODL memecoin. We mention that only because it's part of the record, not as an endorsement, and we don't link it.
- **October 2026:** in a BitcoinTalk Q&A thread, the account answered forum members' questions. Among other things, he recalled being in Japan at the time and paying on-chain "for everyday items and food."[^12] When asked when someone should stop hodling, he joked: "this is investment advice. i am clearly a professional."[^12]

We can't independently verify who controls a pseudonymous forum account, so everything in this section is attributed to the posts themselves.

## What does HODL mean today?

Today HODL is shorthand for a buy-and-hold approach: you keep your coins through ups and downs instead of trying to time the market. CoinDesk describes hodlers as staying invested and not trading when the price falls.[^5] Dictionary.com's definition now covers "other digital assets, or stocks" as well as crypto.[^8] Lopp adds a second sense: if you're investing in bitcoin, it "should be for a long timeframe rather than for day trading."[^7]

It's also a word with a sense of humour. It came from a typo, it took off in a crash, and it caught on because it was honest. GameKyuubi's whole argument was that he wasn't good enough to beat the traders.[^1] That's culture, not advice, and we're not telling anyone what to do with their coins.
